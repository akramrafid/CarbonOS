## 1. Background & Purpose

Location-based Scope 2 accounting under the GHG Protocol requires the **average** emission rate of the electricity grid from which power is drawn. No official annual grid-average factor was officially published by the Department of Environment (DoE) or BPDB for corporate reporting, and the legacy 0.550 kgCO2e/kWh figure frequently cited was a Clean Development Mechanism (CDM Tool 07) combined margin, which is strictly prohibited for Scope 2 corporate accounting under GHG Protocol Scope 2 Guidance.

To provide audit-grade, cited accounting, CarbonOS derives the national grid-average emission rate using two independent methodologies with Monte Carlo uncertainty propagation.

## 2. Method A (Primary): Top-Down National Fuel Balance

Method A calculates total greenhouse gas emissions generated by all power plants feeding the national grid divided by net national electricity generation:

$$\text{Factor}_{\text{Method A}} = \frac{\sum_{\text{fuels}} \text{Emissions}_{\text{domestic}} + \text{Emissions}_{\text{imports}}}{\text{Net Generation}_{\text{national}} + \text{Imports}}$$

- **Natural Gas**: Petrobangla sales to the grid-connected power sector. **Captive industrial generation is strictly excluded**, as factory captive generators represent the company's own Scope 1 fuel consumption. Gas volume (MMscf) is converted to Net Calorific Value (TJ) using Petrobangla standard gas chromatography reference conditions.
- **Liquid Fuels**: Bangladesh Petroleum Corporation (BPC) fuel sales to power (High Speed Diesel and Heavy Fuel Oil).
- **Coal**: Operator reports from major grid-connected coal plants (Barapukuria, Payra 1320 MW, Rampal 1320 MW, Matarbari 1200 MW).
- **Cross-Border Imports**: Power imported from India across Bheramara and Cumilla interconnectors (NVVN, PTC) evaluated at the Indian Central Electricity Authority (CEA) CO2 Baseline Database weighted average; power from Adani Godda evaluated at its plant-specific supercritical coal factor.
- **Emission Factors & GWP**: 2006 IPCC Guidelines Chapter 2 stationary combustion defaults, combined using IPCC AR6 100-year GWP metrics.

## 3. Method B (Cross-Check): Bottom-Up Generation by Technology

Method B acts as a bottom-up cross-check:
- Aggregates plant-by-plant gross and net generation from BPDB Annual Reports (pp. 116–120).
- Maps each plant to installed technology (Combined Cycle Gas Turbine, Open Cycle, Steam Turbine, HFO Engine, Subcritical Coal, Supercritical Coal, Hydro, Solar).
- Multiplies generation by technology heat-rate ranges (kJ/kWh) from cited benchmarks in `heat_rates.json`.
- **Status Rule**: If the gap between Method A and Method B is $\le 5\%$, the fiscal year derivation is marked `DRAFT_REVIEWABLE`. A gap $> 5\%$ without a written technical justification keeps the factor `DRAFT_METHOD_GAP` and blocks ledger approval.

## 4. Monte Carlo Uncertainty Propagation

Uncertainty is evaluated via 10,000 Monte Carlo draws:
- Triangular distributions over heat-rate ranges.
- Gaussian distributions over fuel volumes ($\pm 1\%$ to $\pm 3\%$) and IPCC emissions factors ($\pm 2\%$ to $\pm 50\%$).
- Evaluates central value, 2.5th percentile ($P_{2.5}$), and 97.5th percentile ($P_{97.5}$).
- Yields a typical combined uncertainty of $\pm 2.5\%$ to $\pm 2.8\%$ at the 95% confidence interval.
- **Dual-Control Governance**: Any derived grid factor row is created with status **`DRAFT`** in `esg_emission_factor`. A platform operator other than the author must approve it under dual control before it can be used in an audit-grade sealed ledger calculation.
